How to Buy a Domain Someone Already Owns

To buy a domain someone already owns, you: (1) confirm it's registered and find the owner via WHOIS, (2) value the domain so you know a fair price, (3) make a first offer — usually anonymously — (4) negotiate, (5) close through a licensed escrow service like Escrow.com, and (6) transfer the domain to your registrar. Most owners will sell for the right price; a buy-side broker handles the whole process and keeps you anonymous so the price doesn't jump.

Can you buy a domain that's already taken?

Yes. Almost every registered domain is for sale at some price, even if there's no "for sale" sign on it. A domain that resolves to a live site, a parked page, or nothing at all can still be acquired — you just have to reach the owner and agree on a price.

The 6 steps to buy a domain someone owns

  1. Confirm it's registered and find the owner. Run a WHOIS/owner lookup. Many owners hide behind privacy, so you may need a reverse-WHOIS or broker research to reach a real person.
  2. Value it first. Get an appraisal and comparable sales before you make an offer, so you don't overpay or lowball into silence.
  3. Make the first offer anonymously. If the owner learns a motivated company wants the name, the price climbs. Buyers who stay anonymous consistently pay less — the #1 reason to use a broker.
  4. Negotiate. Expect a counter. Anchor realistically, move in sensible increments, and don't reveal urgency.
  5. Close with escrow. Never wire money directly to a stranger. Use Escrow.com: the buyer funds escrow, the seller transfers the domain, escrow releases payment only after transfer.
  6. Transfer to your registrar and lock it.

What does it cost to buy a domain someone owns?

Prices range from a few hundred dollars for an obscure name to six and seven figures for premium one-word .coms. The three biggest price factors are keyword value, extension (.com commands the most), and how badly the owner wants to keep it. An appraisal before you offer is what keeps you from overpaying.

Should you use a broker to buy a taken domain?

Use a broker when the domain matters to your business, when the owner is hidden or unresponsive, or when you want to stay anonymous so the price stays grounded. Frequently asked questions

Can I force an owner to sell a domain?
No. A domain is private property; you can only make an offer. The one exception is a trademark dispute via UDRP, which is a legal process, not a purchase.
How do I find who owns a domain?
Start with a WHOIS lookup. If it's privacy-protected, a reverse-WHOIS search or a broker's owner-research tools can surface the real registrant.
Is it safe to buy a domain from a stranger?
Only through escrow. Escrow.com holds your payment until the domain is transferred, so neither side can be scammed.
How long does it take?
A cooperative owner can close in days; a negotiation with a hard-to-reach owner can take weeks to months.
What if the owner won't respond?
Persistence and multiple channels help; this is exactly where a broker's research and outreach matter most.
