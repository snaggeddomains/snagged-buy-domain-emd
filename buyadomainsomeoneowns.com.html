Buy a Domain Someone Owns

To buy a domain someone owns, make the owner an offer and agree on a price — then close through an escrow service so the transfer is safe. The smartest way is to stay anonymous: the moment an owner knows a real company wants the name, the price goes up. A buy-side broker contacts the owner on your behalf, hides your identity, negotiates, and handles escrow and transfer.

Why buy anonymously?

Owners price domains based on who's asking. An individual asking about a name gets one number; a funded startup whose product is literally that word gets a much bigger one. Staying anonymous — the core reason buyers use brokers — keeps the negotiation grounded in the domain's value, not your budget.

The process, start to finish

  1. Identify the real owner (WHOIS, reverse-WHOIS, or broker research if privacy-shielded).
  2. Appraise the domain so your offer is anchored to real comparable sales.
  3. Open anonymously with a credible first offer.
  4. Negotiate toward a fair close.
  5. Escrow — buyer funds Escrow.com; seller transfers; escrow releases payment.
  6. Transfer and lock at your registrar.

What will it cost?

The purchase price depends on the name (keyword value, .com vs. other extensions, owner's attachment). A broker typically charges a success fee or a modest upfront + success fee — and usually saves more than it costs by negotiating anonymously and from real comps.

Frequently asked questions

Is every domain for sale?
Practically, yes — most owners will sell at the right price, even without a "for sale" page.
How do I pay safely?
Through escrow (Escrow.com). Never wire funds directly to an unknown seller.
Can the owner just say no?
They can, but a credible, well-researched offer from an anonymous buyer gets far more owners to the table than a cold email revealing who you are.
Can Snagged buy it for me?
Yes — that's the buy-side service: we find the owner, stay anonymous, negotiate, and close.
